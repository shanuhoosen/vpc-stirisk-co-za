Five recurring gaps we see across the market

Installed, but not fully functional

Fire systems are often present, but faults, isolations, degraded coverage or poor condition reduce real protection.

Work completed, but poorly evidenced

Drawings, certificates, inspection records and as-built information are often missing, outdated or disconnected from the physical site.

Buildings evolve; fire systems do not

Changes in occupancy, layout, storage, machinery or HVAC often happen without a matching review of the fire-risk position.

Defects are found, but not closed

Corrective actions frequently lack ownership, target dates, verification and proof that the original risk was truly resolved.

Insurance, compliance and delivery sit in silos

Insurer requirements, technical interpretation, CAPEX, procurement and contractor delivery are rarely managed as one coherent programme.

Fire risk becomes expensive when the process is fragmented.

Most organisations don't have a fire-contractor problem. They have a fire-management problem. Insurance requirements, technical decisions, budgets, procurement, maintenance and verification are handled by different people, with no single operating process connecting them.

The consequence: work gets done, but confidence stays low.

You can spend significant money and receive certificates, yet still be asking whether the requirement was correct, whether the solution was appropriate, whether procurement compared like for like, and whether the work genuinely closed the risk.
